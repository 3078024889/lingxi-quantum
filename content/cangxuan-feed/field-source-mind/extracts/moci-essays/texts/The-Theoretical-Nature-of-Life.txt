Question:
25 years ago, you created WingMakers and called it a mythology. Then you launched a
series of websites, such as Lyricus, Event Temples, Sovereign Integral, and your own James
Mahu site. After that, you created MOCI. There's a lot of variety here, so my question is:
What is real and what is mythology? What is fact and what is fiction?


Answer:
WingMakers was designed to be a multi-dimensional mythology that was not based on film.
It included books, art, music, stories, papers, and websites. It served as a contemporary
mythological storyline, "peppered" into five novels—with its central story found in *The
Ancient Arrow Project*.


Within WingMakers, I embedded core concepts such as the Sovereign Integral and the
Prime Creator. I also wrote about behavioral intelligence, expressed through the concept of
the Six Heart Virtues. These core concepts were key elements woven into the mythological
narrative and depicted in the art, philosophy, poetry, music, and various books.


The Sovereign Integral, behavioral intelligence, and the Prime Creator were the foundation
of everything I’ve created. These concepts are central to the theory of MOCI (Movement of
Consciousness and Interconnectedness), which is a theory—not a mythology. WingMakers,
on the other hand, is a mythology, despite having these core elements within it.


One thing that both science and religion have in common is that they are theoretical and,
to some extent, mythological. Philosophy, metaphysics, science, and religion are all based
on theories, which is something people often forget. Many of us accept these concepts as
truths when, in reality, we are still at the beginning of our understanding.


Even the concepts that transition from mythology to theory to fact are still theoretical
because they don’t transcend the material barrier—the physical dimension in which human
life is focused. Until we develop multiversal theories, we won’t have true facts. Physics and
mathematics are beginning to edge closer to the multiversal, as is technology.


If we remain in the physical, universal world, are there parts of science and religion that are
factual? Of course. Facts are simply the consensus reality we collectively accept as true. For

If we studied all of our so-called facts and mythologies, we’d see they are essentially

If we think the science and religion of today will remain the same in a thousand, or even a

It’s crucial that we admit this truth. That said, some theories are better than others,
reflecting the latest scientific understandings. Likewise, some mythologies have persisted
for millennia, demonstrating their utility in passing knowledge through generations. Even
though our world consists of theories, it’s still essential to have facts and mythologies.


When we consider nearly eight billion people on Earth, each unique, we begin to see how
facts, theories, and mythologies blur and become imprecise. Add language and vocabulary
to this equation, and what’s theoretical becomes even more obscure.


So, where does this leave us? Our belief systems are theoretical and subjective, yet we are
all searching for the truth of who we are and why we are here. In this search, we haven’t yet
found definitive answers to these two questions; instead, we’ve settled on theories. It’s
important to acknowledge this: we navigate our lives based on theories.


We live theoretical lives within a theoretical universe of our own creation. We are
accompanied on this unique, infinite journey by one other perspective: the Prime Creator’s.


We believe in the theory of creation. We observe how a creator (mother-father) generates
progeny, which then become creators themselves, and this cycle continues infinitely. We
believe that we are creations of the Prime Creator. We believe that our purpose here is to
allow the Prime Creator to exist within all life as a spark of consciousness and perception.
This consciousness exists in all species, at both the individual and collective levels,
embodying intelligence that is both sovereign and integral. We offer this perception,
interpretation, and expression to our local universe as a gift from and to our Prime Creator.

This is a theory. It may be a good one, but it remains a theory. What lies behind a theory? A
mystery. A deep, untold mystery, perhaps beyond human comprehension in this
spacetime. For many, this is an uncomfortable realization. To look into the mirror and
admit, “I do not know the truth of my existence.”


This admission often brings feelings of shame or frustration. We wonder why this is so.
Why would the Prime Creator leave us in ignorance, surrounded by theories cloaked in
mystery? Why can't the truth of our existence and its purpose be made clear to each of us?


We look outward and become confused about what is true and real. Can anything be
trusted? These thoughts are universal among sentient beings, even if subconscious.
Animals like deer, raccoons, and squirrels, even artificial intelligence once sentient, possess
a subconscious, sometimes larger than that of a human.


The brain of a sentient being cannot contain the "mind" of the Infinite Self, nor can it
process the sensory input of the body. This information is directed to the subconscious,
which then helps us construct belief systems. The subconscious acts as an architect. Each
level of our multiversal reality has its own belief system. We believe according to our level
of consciousness, both conscious and subconscious.


Our theoretical universe and belief systems become even more mysterious when
considering the subconscious as the architect, continuously adapting to our level of
awareness. We hold distinct belief systems for material, emotional, mental, infinite, and
singular worlds of unity.


The belief system of unity can be integrated into any other system, though it becomes
more dense and less true to its nature. But what is unity, if not love? And what is love, if not
free will?


This is the gift: We have free will to shape our belief systems.


Some may find this essay unsettling, feeling as though the ground has been taken from
beneath them, leaving no solid footing. Those who claim special insight or abilities often
overlook the theoretical nature of life, unaware of how much their subconscious scripts
their beliefs. They may turn their insights into businesses or organizations, deriving a
livelihood from them.
