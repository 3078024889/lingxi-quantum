# Romance Magnetism Demand Brief

Date: 2026-08-11
Status: product input, not report prose
Scope: current US and UK evidence plus studies with North American and European participants; mainland China and other markets still require cultural calibration

## Evidence

- A Pew Research Center survey of 6,034 US adults found mixed online-dating outcomes and substantial unwanted behavior. This supports chapters on refusal, boundary recognition, safety, and recovery after unwanted contact rather than a one-dimensional attraction score. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2023/02/02/from-looking-for-love-to-swiping-the-field/)
- A 2025 profile-perception study with 180 dating-profile owners and 196 judges found that people actively manage both accuracy and positivity in self-presentation. This supports separating authentic expression from optimized presentation instead of treating visibility as inherently healthy. [PubMed 41230894](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41230894/)
- A study of 395 dating-app users associated attachment anxiety with a broad range of app-use motives and a lower likelihood of meeting matches. This supports non-diagnostic nodes about approach hesitation, reassurance seeking, and the gap between matching and real-world contact. [PubMed 32345053](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32345053/)
- An experimental dating-app study with 128 participants found that social-anxiety symptoms changed emotional responses to match-rate feedback and were associated with lower contact initiation. This supports rejection-recovery and initiation mechanisms, but does not justify diagnosing anxiety from birth data. [PubMed 39470693](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39470693/)
- Current UK regulatory guidance identifies fraud, intimate-image abuse, harassment, and grooming as material dating-service risks. Lingxi does not operate matching today, but any future social feature must treat safety controls as architecture rather than advice text. [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/dating-and-social-discovery-know-the-online-safety-risks-rules-and-how-to-comply)

## Product Needs

The report should help a user examine:

1. how presence becomes visible without performing a false self;
2. whether interest signals are authentic and readable;
3. how attention moves into reciprocal contact;
4. how uncertainty changes pacing, withdrawal, or over-pursuit;
5. how refusal is recognized and both parties' boundaries are protected;
6. how low response or rejection is processed without becoming identity;
7. whether depth, novelty, stability, or autonomy is being confused with compatibility;
8. how attraction is sustained through reciprocity rather than intensity alone;
9. what observable behavior would disprove each interpretation;
10. one bounded practice that does not manipulate another person.

## Safety Boundary

- Never predict when love will appear or whether a specific person is destined.
- Never infer a clinical attachment style, anxiety disorder, trauma history, sexuality, or risk status from birth data.
- Never recommend persistence after refusal, covert persuasion, jealousy tactics, surveillance, or emotional dependency.
- Treat the traditional chart marker as an optional cultural lens, not scientific evidence or a measure of desirability.
- Low visibility or expression scores describe current pathways, not worth, attractiveness, or relationship eligibility.

## Chapter Coverage

- Presence: visibility without performance.
- Expression: readable and authentic signals.
- Openness: uncertainty tolerance and pacing.
- Self-assurance: initiation, refusal, and rejection recovery.
- Resonance: depth, reciprocity, and sustainable emotional load.
- Hidden attraction: strengths not currently externalized.
- Drain: over-pursuit, over-adaptation, or unbounded emotional labor.
- Path: integrating the strongest and least-used pathways.
- Practice: a consent-respecting real-world experiment.
- Synthesis: evidence, counterevidence, and next review point.
